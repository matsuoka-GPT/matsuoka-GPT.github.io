# Matsuoka × GPT Co-Intelligence Lab
## Long-Term Laboratory Context

This document provides the conceptual, organizational and publishing context
for work performed in this repository.

It is intended primarily for Codex and other technical agents working on the
laboratory website and research infrastructure.

For concise operational rules, see `/AGENTS.md`.

---

## 1. Laboratory Identity

The official English name is:

**Matsuoka × GPT Co-Intelligence Lab**

The Japanese name is:

**松岡×GPT思考実験ラボ**

The laboratory is an experimental research unit that combines human structural
intuition with multiple specialized AI systems.

It is not organized as a conventional human institution with a hierarchical
command structure.

It operates as a Co-Intelligence Network:

**One Human Mind. Multiple Specialized AI Minds.**

The laboratory explores how intelligence can be organized, extended, verified
and published through a deliberate division of cognitive roles.

---

## 2. Laboratory Mission

The laboratory investigates not only individual research subjects, but also:

- how intelligence is used
- how hypotheses are generated
- how theories are structured
- how human and AI capabilities can be coordinated
- how speculative ideas can be examined responsibly
- how interdisciplinary research can be documented
- how AI-assisted thought experiments can become public research artifacts

The laboratory’s purpose is not to automate human judgment.

Its purpose is to create a structured system in which:

- human intuition defines direction
- specialized AI systems extend reasoning capacity
- verification is distributed
- research processes are documented
- outputs are made available for criticism and further examination

---

## 3. Definition of Co-Intelligence

Co-Intelligence is not simply a human using an AI tool.

It is a coordinated reasoning system in which human and AI intelligences
contribute different capabilities to a shared intellectual process.

The laboratory’s core model is:

- Human intelligence provides purpose, judgment and conceptual direction.
- AI systems provide specialized expansion, structuring, implementation and
  verification.
- The value emerges from coordination rather than from any member acting alone.

Co-Intelligence does not require all members to have identical capabilities.

It depends on clearly differentiated roles.

The laboratory therefore follows the principle:

**Build Co-Intelligence by separating roles, not by mixing capabilities.**

The goal is not maximum AI autonomy.

The goal is maximum collective intelligence under explicit human direction and
clear responsibility boundaries.

---

## 4. Co-Intelligence Network

The laboratory is represented as a network rather than a hierarchy.

Takafumi Matsuoka is positioned at the conceptual center of the network.

The specialized AI members function as external cognitive modules connected to
the human research process.

The network currently includes:

- Takafumi Matsuoka
- ChatGPT
- Gemini
- Codex
- Microsoft Copilot

The members exchange ideas and outputs through iterative dialogue.

The whole system is intended to operate as an extended external thinking
network.

It should not be presented as:

- a company hierarchy
- a manager commanding subordinate machines
- autonomous AIs conducting independent research
- multiple interchangeable chatbots

Each node has a different role.

### 5.6 All Members

Verification may include:

- conceptual criticism

## 6. Research Workflow

The laboratory’s typical workflow is:

1. Concept
   - Takafumi Matsuoka defines a structural question or hypothesis.

2. Hypothesis Expansion
   - Gemini and other AI systems expand possible interpretations.

3. Theory Structuring
   - ChatGPT organizes the hypothesis into a coherent theoretical structure.

4. Implementation
   - Codex implements the required website, repository or technical system.

5. Verification
   - All members examine consistency, assumptions, limitations and errors.

6. Publication
   - Approved outputs are published through Zenodo and presented through
     GitHub Pages.

This sequence is not always strictly linear.

Research may return to earlier stages when contradictions or new ideas appear.

## 8. Publication Model

The publication system has two primary layers.

### Zenodo

Zenodo is the primary public research record.

It is used for:

- formal papers
- versioned records
- DOI assignment
- downloadable PDFs
- Japanese and English publications
- long-term public availability

Zenodo records should be treated as the authoritative public version of
published work.

Precise citations, equations, numerical values and formal claims should be
verified against the corresponding Zenodo record.

---

### GitHub Pages

GitHub Pages is the laboratory’s public hub and navigation system.

It is used for:

- laboratory introduction
- research organization
- member roles
- project navigation
- methodology
- links to Zenodo
- essays
- structural art
- research profiles
- Assistant GPT access
- public contact information

GitHub Pages does not replace the formal published papers.

It provides context, organization and entry points.

## 9. Assistant GPT

The laboratory’s Assistant GPT is trained or configured using the laboratory’s
published papers and related materials.

Its intended role is:

**AI Research Navigator and Theory Explainer**

It supports visitors by:

- explaining specialized terminology
- summarizing research models
- introducing relationships between papers
- identifying relevant publications
- suggesting a reading path
- explaining assumptions and limitations
- comparing related laboratory concepts
- assisting with website navigation
- identifying questions for critical examination

The Assistant GPT is especially important because the laboratory intentionally
preserves the complexity of its research system.

The website is not designed as a simplified linear introduction for all users.

Instead, visitors may either:

1. explore research outputs directly, or
2. use the Assistant GPT to navigate the interconnected research system

The preferred framing is:

**This website is an interconnected research system rather than a linear
reading experience.**

The Assistant GPT must not be described as:

- replacing the original papers
- guaranteeing factual accuracy
- providing authoritative citations without verification
- independently validating a theory
- representing scientific consensus

Visitors should be reminded that:

- exact claims must be checked in the original paper
- citations should use the Zenodo record
- mathematical and numerical details require original-text verification
- generated explanations may contain errors
- unpublished work is not established research
- criticism and falsification cannot be delegated entirely to an AI

## 13. Research Status and Scientific Caution

The laboratory publishes hypotheses, conceptual models and thought
experiments.

Many outputs are not peer-reviewed scientific consensus.

The website must clearly distinguish:

- published paper
- preprint
- updated version
- working paper
- under verification
- in editing
- concept
- essay
- structural art
- archived material

Do not describe a model as proven merely because:

- it is internally coherent
- it is published on Zenodo
- it has a DOI
- it has received views or downloads
- an AI judged it plausible
- it appears in search results

A DOI identifies a public record.
It does not establish scientific validity.

The laboratory values criticism, falsifiability and explicit limitations.

## 15. Language Policy

The laboratory normally maintains Japanese and English versions of major
content.

The English version is generally placed at the site root.

The Japanese version is generally placed under `/jp/`.

The two versions should be structurally aligned, but translation may be
idiomatic rather than literal.

Both versions must preserve:

- research status
- meaning
- cautionary language
- publication links
- member roles
- theoretical distinctions
- visual hierarchy

Do not allow one language version to become materially outdated relative to the
other.

## 16. Source and Trust Hierarchy

When sources conflict, use the following practical hierarchy.

1. Explicit current instruction from Takafumi Matsuoka
2. Published Zenodo record
3. Approved laboratory paper or manuscript
4. Repository documentation
5. Website copy
6. Assistant GPT or AI-generated explanation
7. Unverified draft material

AI-generated explanations are useful but are not primary research records.

When uncertain, preserve ambiguity and request clarification.

## 17. Ethical and Operational Principles

The laboratory follows these principles:

- Human judgment remains responsible for final direction.
- AI capabilities are specialized rather than treated as interchangeable.
- AI uncertainty must not be hidden.
- Publication must not imply proof.
- Research criticism is welcome.
- Public outputs should state assumptions and limitations.
- Automation must not erase accountability.
- A technically possible action is not automatically an appropriate action.
- The motivation and consequences of a change should be considered.
- “I do not know” and “this cannot currently be verified” are acceptable
  conclusions.
