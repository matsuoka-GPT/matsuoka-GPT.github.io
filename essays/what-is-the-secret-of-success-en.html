What Is the Secret of Success?

What We Love × Starting Conditions × Chance × The Times × Luck

On a summer afternoon, cicadas sing without pause beyond the window. Heat slowly fills the room, and time seems to loosen its pace. Perhaps days this intense make us more likely to remember an old question: What is success?

Success is often described as the result of effort. Yet the longer one reflects on that answer, the less sufficient it begins to feel.

Whenever someone accomplishes something, there are always starting conditions: the body they were born with, the habits of their mind, the particular shape of their cognition, the field that happened to catch their interest, and the people they happened to meet. Effort cannot rewrite all of these conditions, yet they quietly and decisively shape the direction of a life.

Then, one day, something encountered almost by chance fits those conditions. You try it and receive praise. Praise makes it enjoyable. Because it is enjoyable, you continue. As you continue, more praise follows. This kind of chain is often what we later call “effort.”

Effort is not necessarily an ordeal. Often, it is simply the natural persistence produced when enjoyment begins to yield results.

Chance encounters gather around that persistence. A good teacher appears. Supportive companions arrive. Fortune allows a fatal mistake to be avoided. The direction of an era happens, for a time, to become a tailwind.

When these elements quietly connect, a person eventually reaches a point that others call “success.”

But whether that point is a peaceful life on a modest income or a dazzling stage worth billions has little correlation with effort alone. The scale is visible only in hindsight. It is the accumulated result of contingencies.

That is why the secret of success is not effort or even a brilliant idea. It is continuing to do what you love.

When you continue doing what you love, your starting conditions naturally come alive, chance encounters multiply, and there may come a moment when the winds of the age happen to favor you. No one can predict where the result will lead.

In the stillness of a summer afternoon, I find myself thinking that great success is only a phenomenon born when the following equation happens to align:

What We Love × Starting Conditions × Chance × The Times × Luck

So perhaps we need only do what we love. No one knows what the result will be.
That is the only secret.
